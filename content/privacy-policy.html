Privacy policy

Last updated 29 October 2026

EU JUST helps people explore European workplace-law records and reach their official sources. This policy explains how msoit handles personal data when you visit EU JUST, contact us, or appear in the records we collect.

Controller: msoit, Belgium. For privacy questions or requests, email hello@msoit.eu.

Visiting and searching

Our hosting infrastructure processes connection and request information, including your IP address, browser or user-agent information, requested page, date and time, response status and request duration. We use this information to deliver the website, investigate errors and protect its availability and security. Our lawful basis is our legitimate interest in operating a reliable, secure research website (GDPR Article 6(1)(f)).

The public collection can be browsed without creating an account. We do not use advertising or analytics scripts in the current public site.

In the static public collection, search and filtering run in your browser. Where a research deployment uses server-based search, the terms are sent to EU JUST to retrieve matching records. Search terms and filters can appear in the page URL and browser history. If you reload, open or share that URL, those terms may be sent to the hosting infrastructure as part of the request. Avoid entering private details about yourself or someone else into a public search URL.

People in public records

EU JUST collects and republishes selected information from EUR-Lex, the EU Publications Office and the official sources linked on each record, including EU courts and institutions. This information is obtained from those sources, rather than directly from the people it describes.

Records may include case titles and numbers, dates, courts, names or initials of parties and representatives, and published descriptions of legal disputes. A record may still identify a person when it uses initials. Workplace cases can also concern sensitive subjects, including health or discrimination.

Our purpose is to make European workplace-law sources easier to find and understand. For ordinary personal data, we rely on our legitimate interest in providing source-linked legal research and information to the public (Article 6(1)(f)). Public availability does not remove privacy rights or, where applicable, the additional requirements for sensitive personal data.

The linked official document remains the authority. Pending applications describe allegations, not established findings. If information about you is inaccurate, outdated, unnecessarily identifying or otherwise raises a privacy concern, contact hello@msoit.eu with the EU JUST page and relevant source. You can ask for correction, restriction, removal or consideration of an objection. These rights are assessed in context, including applicable freedom-of-expression and information protections (Articles 16–18, 21 and 85).

AI and external services

AI-provider settings and API-key entry have been removed from the website. You do not need an AI account or API key to browse the public records.

If you choose to open an external AI app and paste a copied research prompt, that is a separate action in your account with that service, under its privacy terms. The response stays in that app.

Where an authorised operator requests a source-based summary in a research deployment, available judgment text and metadata are sent to its configured AI service, and the generated report is stored separately and may appear on a case page. Such reports are labelled as generated interpretation. Removing provider settings does not itself erase previously stored configuration or reports. Those records must be managed separately, and you can contact us about information concerning you.

Research summaries and topic matching do not make decisions about you with legal or similarly significant effects (Article 22).

Cookies

EU JUST uses eujust_cookie_consent to remember a choice made through its cookie-preference control. It has a configured lifetime of 180 days, uses SameSite=Lax and is marked Secure when the site is accessed over HTTPS. It supports the preference control; it is not an advertising or analytics cookie.

No optional analytics or marketing cookies are used by the current public application. You can reopen Cookie preferences in the footer or delete the preference cookie through your browser settings. Deleting it makes the site forget the stored choice. Where a cookie is strictly necessary to provide a preference service you request, it does not require optional-tracking consent under the applicable cookie rules.

Hosting and recipients

Railway hosts the EU JUST website application in its EU West region in Amsterdam, Netherlands. Railway processes request information for delivery, operation and security. Authorised operators can access information needed to run the service. Providers that deliver our email process messages sent to our contact address. If source-based summaries are generated, the configured AI service processes the source information as described above.

Application hosting in Amsterdam does not by itself mean that all support, log processing, subprocessors or AI services operate within the European Economic Area. Railway's published processing and transfer terms are available in its Data Processing Agreement and privacy policy. Contact hello@msoit.eu for information about the arrangements and safeguards applicable to your information. This policy does not represent provider terms as proof that every processing location is in the EEA.

Official-source links take you to independently operated websites. Their operators process your visit under their own privacy information. We may also disclose information where required by a binding legal obligation, relying on Article 6(1)(c) where applicable.

How long information is kept

Cookie preference
The configured cookie lifetime is 180 days. You can delete it earlier in your browser.
Hosting logs
Our Railway Hobby plan provides a seven-day log-history window. Railway states that upgrading a plan can restore older logs; the viewing window is not a guarantee that every underlying copy is erased after seven days. See Railway's retention documentation or contact us for clarification about your information.
Published legal records
Records are retained while relevant and necessary for the research collection. We consider accuracy, source changes, the public information purpose and requests from affected people when deciding whether to correct, restrict or remove a record.
Privacy enquiries and correspondence
We keep correspondence for the time needed to handle the enquiry, document our response and meet applicable legal obligations or resolve related disputes. The period depends on the nature of the request and any continuing obligation or dispute.
Previously saved operator data
Removing the settings interface does not erase previously saved provider configurations or generated reports. These are managed separately from the public interface; retention, provider copies and backups must be considered when responding to a request.

There is no single retention period for all information. You can ask us which period or criteria apply to a particular record or request (Articles 13(2)(a) and 14(2)(a)).

Your rights and complaints

Depending on the processing and applicable conditions, you can request access, correction, erasure, restriction or portability, and object to processing based on legitimate interests (Articles 15–21). Where we rely on consent, you can withdraw it without affecting processing already carried out lawfully. The current preference cookie is not consent to the reuse of court information or to AI analysis.

Email hello@msoit.eu. For requests about a court record, include its EU JUST URL or case reference and explain your concern. We may ask for proportionate information to verify identity if needed. Do not send identity documents or additional sensitive details unless we explain why they are necessary.

We respond without undue delay and normally within one month. If a permitted extension is needed because of the complexity or number of requests, we tell you within the first month and explain the reason. Rights are subject to the relevant legal conditions and exceptions; if we cannot fulfil a request, we explain the decision and available remedies (Article 12).

You can complain to a competent data-protection authority, including the Belgian Autorité de protection des données / Gegevensbeschermingsautoriteit (APD/GBA). You may also have the right to complain in the Member State of your habitual residence, work or the alleged infringement (Article 77).
